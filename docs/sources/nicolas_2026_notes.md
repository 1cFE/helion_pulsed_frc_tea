# Nicolas et al. 2026, source note

T. Nicolas, C. Goupil, E. Herbert and J. Ball, "The thermodynamics of
pulsed magneto-inertial fusion," Journal of Fusion Energy 45:24 (2026).
Reference [16] in the writeup.

## Used in the writeup

**The pulsed gain against a Lawson time** (Section 6.1): their pulsed
gain, fusion energy over plasma energy, is the same ratio the yield
bound uses, measured against their Lawson time rather than Lackner's
breakeven time. Cross-check only; the bound is stated from the energy
and power densities directly.

**The fusion-to-bremsstrahlung reconstruction** (constraint 1, Section
3): 3.6 to 9 times too favorable, against Lackner's about an order of
magnitude.

**Recovery "exceeding 95%"** (Section 6.5): the writeup states that
Helion's FAQ cites this paper for that figure, that the paper gives it
only as an ideal Carnot-form bound, and that the paper sets it aside on
the thin-cycle argument.

## Open: the 97.5% particle recovery

Kirtley's public response to this work (writeup reference [31]) cites
this paper for "up to 97.5% particle recovery efficiency, dependent on
the compression ratio", as grounds for rejecting Slough's 40% ceiling.
Nothing in this study has been checked against that figure, and the
quantity is not obviously the one the writeup prices: f_pdv is the share
of plasma expansion work that reaches the coils (an energy recovery),
while a particle recovery efficiency is a statement about the particles
that return. Three questions for a read of the paper:

1. Does the 97.5% appear, and is it energy or particles?
2. What compression ratio does it require, and how does it compare with
   the ratio each device in this study runs (V_i/V_f under constraint
   6, 0.26 to 0.31 m^3 against chambers of 18 to 106 m^3)?
3. Does it sit inside the same thin-cycle argument the paper uses to set
   aside the "exceeding 95%" figure, or is it a separate result?

Until that read, Section 6.5's "Two published figures sit against it"
describes the two figures this study has verified in those papers, not
the whole of what either paper contains. If the 97.5% is an energy
recovery at a reachable compression ratio, the sentence is incomplete
and that paper supplies a figure for Helion as well as against.
