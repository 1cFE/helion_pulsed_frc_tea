# Helion's own cost-of-electricity claim, and its removal

Source page: https://www.helionenergy.com/faq/ ("How much will Helion's
fusion power cost?"). Archived snapshots read through the Wayback
Machine (web.archive.org) and the live page read directly, all on
2026-10-08. The $10/MWh figure this study investigates is Helion's own,
and the page no longer carries it.

## 2021-08-02 (snapshot 20210802113408), verbatim

"There are four main components of electricity cost: 1) Capital cost
2) Operating cost 3) Up-time 4) Fuel cost. Helion's fusion power plant
is projected to have negligible fuel cost, low operating cost, high
up-time and competitive capital cost because we can do fusion so
efficiently. Helion's levelized cost of electricity is projected to be
less than $0.04 per kWh without assuming any economies of scale from
mass production, carbon credits, or government incentives."

## 2022-12-05 through 2023-12-11, verbatim

"We estimate that Helion's fusion power will be one of the lowest cost
sources of electricity. Helion's cost of electricity production is
projected to be $0.01 per kWh without assuming any economies of scale
from mass production, carbon credits, or government incentives."

Present in snapshots 20221205171905, 20230207110024, 20230601150845 and
20231211021000.

## 2024-12-06 (snapshot 20241206032813) onward

No cost figure on the page.

## Live page, read 2026-10-08

"We estimate that Helion's fusion power will be one of the lowest cost
sources of electricity." The technology page states only "Helion's goal
is reliable, affordable electricity from fusion, delivered to the grid."

## How to apply

The figure is Helion's, stated on its own FAQ, and it moved: less than
$0.04 per kWh in 2021, $0.01 per kWh from late 2022 through 2023, and
withdrawn by the end of 2024, with the qualitative sentence kept and the
number deleted. Quote it as a dated company statement, not as a current
claim, in the same way the writeup handles the withdrawal of "net
electricity" from the Polaris goals.

Two details of the 2022-2023 wording bear on this study's basis. The
claim excludes economies of scale from mass production, carbon credits
and government incentives, which is the same no-subsidy constant-dollar
footing the ladder uses. And the 2021 version says "levelized cost of
electricity" explicitly, so the comparison with an LCOE is Helion's own
framing rather than one imposed here.
